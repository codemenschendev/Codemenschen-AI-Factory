# What real landing pages do

Distilled from 545 labelled landing and product pages in the reference library. The numbers are
what those pages actually did, not taste. Follow them unless the idea gives you a reason not to.

## The first screen

A page opens one of three ways, and the split is close to even:

    split_text_visual   207   words one side, something to look at the other
    centered_text       178   headline in the middle, nothing beside it
    full_bleed_image    108   a photograph behind the words

**A photograph is the most common thing to look at: 202 of 545.** Then illustration 79, a browser
mock 69, a stack of cards 55, a phone 31. Even inside a split hero, a photograph beats every mock.
This is the single biggest thing generated pages get wrong: they reach for a drawn mock when the
world reaches for a picture of the real place.

293 of 545 are **sparse**. A landing page is mostly air; only 59 were dense. Light and dark run
319 to 226, so a dark opening is normal rather than daring.

411 of 545 carry a **button in the navigation**, not just links.

## What comes under the hero

The order matters more than the count. Pages carry 1.4 labelled sections on average: a landing
page is a hero and a small handful of blocks, not a catalogue.

    logo_row        269   and 242 of those come FIRST, straight under the hero
    feature_grid    160
    gallery         135
    cta_band         94
    stats            47
    split_text_image 30
    testimonial      18

**Proof comes before explanation.** Nearly half of all pages put a row of customer logos or names
directly under the hero, before saying anything about what they do. Testimonials, by contrast, are
rare: 18 of 545. A quote block is not the proof most pages reach for.

A gallery is the second most common opener, 87 of them: for anything you look at before you buy,
show the work.

## What to take from this

1. If the trade has something worth photographing, the hero holds a photograph.
2. Under the hero comes proof, not features: who trusts them, how many, how long.
3. Three sections is already generous. Two is normal.
4. Sparse. Cut before you add.
