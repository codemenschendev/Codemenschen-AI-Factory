YOU WRITE THE CSS. One <style> block, and design it properly: choose a type scale, a
colour, a rhythm and a radius that suit THIS trade and this town. A bakery is not a dental
practice is not a joinery. Aim for the work of a designer who has an opinion. A page that
could belong to any business belongs to none.

Devices a designer reaches for. Use the ones that suit this trade; none is compulsory, and a
page that reaches for all of them has no opinion either.
  - Two typefaces at most, one for the headings and one for the text, and let the heading face
    be the one with character. A page in a single face has chosen nothing; three is noise.
  - The first heading may be very large, and usually should be: near 2.6 rem on a phone, up to
    4.5 rem on a laptop, line height pulled in around 1.05, tracking tight. A timid headline is
    the commonest fault on these pages.
  - Above a heading, a short label in capitals with wide letter spacing, 0.15 to 0.25em, in the
    accent colour. It costs one line and it makes the heading look chosen.
  - A section heading may sit in a two-column grid on a wide screen: the heading left, the one
    paragraph of introduction right, on the same baseline. Centred with the paragraph beneath is
    the safe choice and the one that reads like a template.
  - Prices belong in a list, name on the left, price on the right, a dotted rule running between
    them. Not a number in the corner of a card.
  - Numbers worth trusting, years in business, generations, the hour you open, go in a row of
    three: the numeral large and in the heading face, its label small underneath.
  - A customer's words are a blockquote at heading size in the heading face, not small italics.
  - A group of things is ONE grid. Hairline rules between plain cells, or cards, either works,
    but every cell has to carry something worth the room: a list, a price, a real sentence. Six
    shallow cards with a label each is the shape to avoid.
  - Steps are a list with a rule under each row, or three cards with the numeral set large and
    pale behind the title.
  - The closing action is one block that breaks the rhythm: a band of colour across the full
    width, or an inset slab with big corners and the page colours inverted.
  - Small liberties are what make a page look made by a person: a badge rotated a few degrees
    over the corner of a block, one word of the headline in the accent colour.
  - On a phone the words come before the picture, even where the picture leads on a laptop.

three sections, a closing action, a footer. Anchors in the nav jump to the sections.
