# 07 — Social Proof Strategy (Zero Traction)

## The problem, stated honestly

At launch we have: no buyers, no testimonials, no logos, no review scores. Inventing any of these is banned and would be existentially stupid — this audience's scam detector is our biggest conversion barrier (see 04). 

## The strategy: substitute *proof of process* for *proof of popularity*

Popularity proof says "others trusted us." Process proof says "you can verify us yourself." For a category-creating product, process proof is arguably stronger — early adopters of new categories are exactly the people who check.

Three pillars:

## Pillar 1 — Published validation methodology

A standalone, versioned public document ("How we validate apps, v1.0") that every listing links to. Contents:

- Test design: how long (e.g. 6 weeks), how users were recruited (channels, ad spend disclosed), what they paid, what counts as a "paying user" (real money, own card, no incentives/refund-for-signup).
- Pass/fail criteria: what threshold an app must clear to be listed — and the explicit statement that many apps we build **fail this test and are never listed**.
- Evidence standard per listing: anonymized payment records / analytics screenshots, PII removed; date-stamped.
- Limits, stated by us first: "A successful test is evidence of demand at that moment. It is not a forecast. Apps that validated can still fail."
- Changelog: methodology versions are dated and diffs kept public.

Why it works: it converts our weakest claim ("trust our numbers") into an auditable artifact, and pre-empts objection #9 (04-customer-psychology).

## Pillar 2 — Build-in-public ("Die Werkstatt" / "The Workshop Log")

A dated, ongoing log — blog + newsletter (+ optionally LinkedIn/X), DE+EN. Cadence: minimum 2 posts/month; consistency matters more than volume.

**What we publish:**
- Each validation run: what we built, the test setup, and the actual result — including failures. *Publishing failures is the flagship move*: nothing signals honesty more cheaply or credibly, and it makes the listed apps' numbers believable by contrast.
- Platform-building itself: pricing decisions, contract drafting (what counsel made us change), how the dashboard works. Meta-transparency.
- Operational events, factually: "App X was licensed this week." "App Y's roadmap milestone shipped." Later: "Our first resale — here's exactly how the 5% handover worked."

**Hard rules:**
- Historical actuals only; no projections, no "this one's going to be big."
- No buyer identity without written consent; buyer stories are process-factual (what they bought, how onboarding went), never earnings-flavored.
- Failure posts get the same production quality as success posts.

**Distribution:** newsletter doubles as the waitlist nurture (01-website-strategy); posts feed the SEO educational cluster (10-seo-strategy).

## Pillar 3 — Verifiable identity and openness assets

- Codemenschen.at cross-link, named team with photos, Vienna address, Impressum — see 06.
- **Public standard contract**: the single most "a scammer would never do this" artifact. Linked pre-purchase. [COUNSEL REVIEW]
- Public FAQ that leads with the hard objections ("You don't own the IP — here's why", "Yes, apps can fail").
- Open metrics page (optional, month 2+): count of apps built, validated, failed, listed, licensed — running totals, all historical. No revenue figures.

## What we never do (restated as policy)

- No invented or "composite" testimonials, no borrowed logos, no fake review widgets, no "as seen in" without a real feature, no purchased followers/engagement, no "1,200 people viewing this" mechanics.
- No case study contains numbers that aren't historical actuals; none appears on marketing pages if it's ad-performance data (dashboard only).

## Graduation path

As real proof accrues, add it in this order, keeping process transparency running underneath:
1. First licensed app → factual announcement + (with consent) buyer onboarding story.
2. First roadmap cycle delivered → before/after roadmap post.
3. First failure or resale handled → full honest case study (see 06, risk #9 — pre-write the playbook).
4. Only then: quote-style testimonials, strictly process-experiential ("the terms were exactly as published"), never outcome-financial.

**KPI for this strategy:** return-visitor rate and newsletter growth (proxy for "watching us before trusting us"), plus the support-question rate on authenticity objections trending down.
