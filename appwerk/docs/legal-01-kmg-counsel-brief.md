# Counsel Brief — Regulatory Classification of Appwerk Licenses (Austria)

> **Status:** DRAFT prepared by the founding team for Austrian capital-markets counsel.
> **This is not legal advice.** It is a structured statement of facts and questions so counsel can answer them efficiently. Nothing launches until these questions are answered in writing.

## 1. The product, stated precisely

- Codemenschen.at ("the Company", Vienna) builds prototype apps and validates them with real paying users during a fixed, documented test window.
- A consumer ("the Licensee") purchases an **exclusive, revenue-bearing license** to one app for **€500–€5,000** (one-time). The Licensee is the sole licensee of that app.
- The Licensee pays a **monthly retainer of 5–10% of the purchase price**, funding ongoing development and operations under a roadmap the Licensee approves.
- All revenue generated by the app flows to the Licensee. The Company retains the code and all IP and is the **exclusive development provider** (the Licensee cannot move development elsewhere).
- Optional, separately funded **advertising budget** at the Licensee's discretion.
- **Exit:** resale of the license to another buyer on the Company's platform; 5% handover fee.
- **Default:** on retainer non-payment, the license is suspended and the app relisted; on resale, the former Licensee receives proceeds net of outstanding retainer amounts.
- Marketing explicitly makes **no return promises, no projections**, discloses failure risk prominently, and avoids all investment framing.
- Offered publicly online to consumers in Austria/Germany (DE) and EU-wide (EN), fully self-serve.

## 2. Questions for counsel

### A. Kapitalmarktgesetz (KMG 2019)
1. Does the exclusive revenue-bearing license constitute a **Veranlagung** within the meaning of § 1 Abs 1 Z 3 KMG (property right conferring claims to earnings, without the investor's own management, not securitized)?
2. If yes: which **prospectus exemption** is realistically available (e.g. § 3 KMG small-offering thresholds — total consideration limits per 12 months), and what ongoing counting/monitoring obligations follow?
3. Does the Licensee's roadmap-approval right constitute sufficient "own management" (*eigene Verwaltung*) to take the product outside the Veranlagung definition — and if so, how much decision power must be real, documented, and non-delegable for that position to hold?

### B. Adjacent regimes
4. Alternative Finanzierungsgesetz (AltFG) — applicable or excluded?
5. MiFID II / WAG 2018 — any risk of the license qualifying as a transferable security or other financial instrument, particularly given the **on-platform resale market** (does operating the resale venue create an MTF-adjacent problem)?
6. Gewerbeordnung / any licensing requirement for operating the resale marketplace and charging the 5% handover fee?
7. AML/KYC: at €500–5,000 tickets with resale, what customer-due-diligence obligations attach, if any?

### C. Consumer law interfaces (see also legal-02)
8. Confirmation of the FAGG withdrawal-waiver mechanism drafted in `legal-02-terms-draft.md` (§ 18 FAGG express-consent route) for this specific product structure.
9. Unfair-terms (§ 879 ABGB, KSchG) review of the suspension/relisting/net-proceeds mechanic — drafted in plain language in legal-02; is the substance defensible, and what notice/cure periods are required?
10. UCPD/UWG review of the approved marketing framings: "license and back your own app", "your own app in the store", "revenue rights". Which, if any, cross into misleading territory given the license-not-ownership structure?

### D. Structure alternatives (only if A/B answers are bad)
11. If the license is a Veranlagung with no workable exemption: assess (a) capping annual offering volume under the small-offering exemption, (b) restructuring the roadmap-approval right into genuine management participation, (c) an information-document regime instead of full prospectus.

## 3. Facts counsel should NOT assume
- There are **no earnings projections** anywhere in marketing or checkout. Historical validation data only.
- The withdrawal position is the express-waiver route, **not** the "custom-made goods" exemption (the apps pre-exist their buyer; the team considers the custom-made argument unavailable and does not rely on it).
- No traction exists at launch; all listing data shown pre-launch is labeled placeholder.
