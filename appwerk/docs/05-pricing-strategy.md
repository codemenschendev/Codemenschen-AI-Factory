# 05 — Pricing Strategy (Presentation)

Scope: how prices are *presented*. The commercial structure is fixed: €500–5,000 exclusive license, 5–10%/month retainer, optional separate ad budget, 5% resale handover fee, suspension/relisting on non-payment.

## Principles

1. **Total-cost honesty, up front.** We show the full first-year cost ourselves before the buyer computes it suspiciously. A buyer who discovers the retainer math at checkout is a withdrawal waiting to happen.
2. **Every euro has a named job.** Price = the exclusive license. Retainer = development + operation under the roadmap. Ad budget = optional promotion, spent on ads, reported as historical actuals in the dashboard. Resale fee = handover work. No unexplained line items.
3. **Anchoring by honest comparison, never by fabrication.** No fake strikethrough prices, no "worth €20,000". The legitimate anchor is the real-world alternative cost.
4. **No value promises in pricing copy.** Price is what it costs, never what it "returns."

## Presenting the purchase price (€500–5,000)

- Show per-app, plain: "Exclusive license: €2,400 (one-time)."
- Legitimate anchor (verifiable, no forecast): "Commissioning a comparable app from an agency typically costs €15,000–50,000 — and starts with zero users. This app exists and has been paid for by real users." Keep the range defensible from Codemenschen's own agency price list.
- Explain the range honestly: price reflects build scope and validation strength — not projected earnings. Explicitly: "The price is not a valuation and implies nothing about future revenue."
- Address the "why so cheap" objection right on the pricing page (see 04, objection 12): early, small, risky — priced accordingly.

## Presenting the retainer (5–10%/month of purchase price)

The retainer is the most attack-able number. Present it as the product, not a fee.

- Always in euros AND percent: "€120/month (5% of your license price)."
- Always with the total: "Over 12 months that's €1,440 on top of your €2,400 license. We show you this because you'd calculate it anyway." That sentence — showing the math first — is the single highest-trust move on the page.
- What it buys, concretely: the agreed roadmap (listed per app), hosting/operation, maintenance, updates. Link the actual roadmap from every listing.
- Why it varies (5% vs 10%): roadmap intensity/operating cost of that app — stated per listing.
- Suspension terms in plain language, adjacent, not hidden: "If the retainer goes unpaid, the license is suspended and the app is relisted. If it resells, you receive the resale surplus minus outstanding retainer." [COUNSEL REVIEW: unfair-terms review of the suspension/forfeiture mechanic; EU consumer law.]

## Presenting the ad budget (optional)

- Clearly optional, clearly separate: "If you want us to promote the app beyond the included work, you can set a monthly ad budget. 100% is spent on ads; spend and results are reported in your dashboard."
- Historical actuals only, dashboard only. Marketing pages may say ads *exist* as an option; they never show performance numbers, benchmarks, or "typical results."
- No minimums pressure, no "recommended budget for growth" language (that's a veiled forecast).

## Presenting resale and exit (5% fee)

- Frame: "There is a door. Here's exactly how it opens." Exit mechanics are a trust asset — lead with them, don't concede them.
- Plain terms: relist on the platform at a price you choose; on handover we take 5% and transfer the license to the new buyer.
- Mandatory honesty line: "Resale is not guaranteed. Your app may sell for more, less, or not find a buyer." No secondary-market statistics until we have real ones (then: historical actuals only).

## Page-level layout (Pricing & Terms page)

1. One worked example, full stack, fictional-but-labeled app: license €2,400 + retainer €120/mo (5%) + optional ads + 5% on resale → first-year total shown in one box.
2. "Every cost on this platform" table — the complete list, with the sentence: "There are no other fees."
3. Suspension & failure terms in plain language (mirrored in AGB) [COUNSEL REVIEW].
4. EU 14-day withdrawal right, stated plainly; we default to honoring it [COUNSEL REVIEW: interaction with immediately-provided digital services].
5. Link to the full standard contract (see 06-trust): the contract itself is public.

## Anchoring without manipulation — rules

Allowed: agency-build cost comparison (verifiable); showing €500 apps next to €5,000 apps (real range); first-year total cost shown by us first.
Banned: strikethroughs/"discounts", decoy tiers, "value €X", any cost-vs-revenue comparison, countdown pricing, "price increases soon", bundling ad budget to obscure totals.
