# 04 — Customer Psychology

## Audience profile

**Who:** Non-technical, financially comfortable individuals (DACH first, EN second). They already allocate to ETFs and/or real estate; they read about AI weekly and feel they're watching the software economy from the outside. Disposable income comfortably covers €500–5,000 plus a small monthly commitment. Age skew 30–55. Zero coding ability and — importantly — zero desire to acquire it.

**Jobs to be done:**
1. Functional: get hands-on exposure to a software product without building or operating anything.
2. Emotional: feel like a participant in the software/AI economy instead of a spectator; have "my app" to point to.
3. Social: a genuinely interesting thing to talk about — more concrete than an ETF, more respectable than crypto.

**What they are NOT:** operators, acquirers, or yield-hunters. Anyone arriving with an Acquire.com due-diligence mindset is a secondary persona; anyone hunting passive income is a persona we must actively disqualify with honest copy (a mis-sold buyer becomes a withdrawal, a suspension, or a public complaint).

**Prior mental models they'll reach for (and our corrections):**
- "Like buying a small business" → No: license, not business, not IP.
- "Like Kickstarter" → No: the app already exists and was paid for by users.
- "Like an investment product" → No, and we never use investment analogies. It's a license to back a product, with real risk of loss.

## Objections (master list)

Ranked by expected frequency × deal-killing power. Full answers in 08-objection-handling.

1. **"I don't really own it."** Correct — and the site must say so proudly, not defensively. Reframe: you own the exclusive license and revenue rights; the studio owning the IP is why it keeps getting built. Own-nothing suspicion is the #1 comprehension risk.
2. **"Lock-in: I can only use you for development."** True. Frame as the deal's logic: one accountable studio, agreed roadmap, and the retainer that funds it. Exit exists: resale on-platform (5% fee).
3. **"What if the app fails?"** It can. State loss plainly; show the exact failure/exit mechanics; show that validation reduces but does not remove risk.
4. **"Why are there no humans to talk to?"** Deliberate: self-serve keeps prices at €500–5,000 instead of consulting rates; everything a salesperson would tell you is published; support exists post-purchase and for written questions.
5. **"Is this even legal / is it an investment?"** [COUNSEL REVIEW] Copy states clearly what it is (license) and isn't (security/equity); Austrian company, Impressum, EU withdrawal right honored.
6. **"Why would you sell it if it works?"** Our business is building and retainers at scale, not operating a portfolio. Honest and checkable via codemenschen.at.
7. **"What exactly does the retainer buy?"** Agreed roadmap, published per app; suspension terms if unpaid.
8. **"Can I resell? What's it worth later?"** Resale on-platform, 5% fee; no promises about resale value — could be more, less, or find no buyer.
9. **"How do I know the validation numbers are real?"** Published methodology, raw evidence per listing, build-in-public log (see 07).
10. **"What happens if Codemenschen disappears?"** [COUNSEL REVIEW: license continuity / escrow-style provisions] — must have a written answer before launch.
11. **"5–10% monthly of purchase price — that's a lot over time."** Show the math ourselves, first (see 05-pricing).
12. **"Why is it so cheap if users already pay for it?"** Because it's early, small, and risky — the price is honest about that. Cheapness is a risk signal we explain, not hide.

## Psychological purpose per homepage section

Mirrors 03-messaging section map; this table is the "why" behind each beat.

| Section | Psychological job | Mechanism | Failure mode to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Pattern-interrupt: open a category slot in their head | Novel-but-plain claim; zero hype so the novelty itself carries | Sounding like every "earn online" pitch — instant pattern-match to scam |
| How it works | Convert surprise into coherence ("makes complete sense") | Step logic with the money flows visible; naming what it's NOT | Vagueness; hiding retainer until checkout |
| The studio | Transfer competence trust ("they solved the hard part") | Real names, Vienna, codemenschen.at track record, methodology link | Stock photos, vague "our experts" |
| What you never do | Collapse the capability objection ("I don't need developers") | Enumerate the scary technical tasks and cross them out | Implying effortlessness of OUTCOMES (that's passive-income territory); only effort is removed, not risk |
| Backing + risk | Make ownership-of-role feel real AND survivable ("I could actually do this") | Dashboard preview + the risk block treated as first-class content — honesty at the moment of self-projection is what makes the projection safe | Burying risk; any number that smells like a forecast |
| Catalog preview | Convert desire into a next step ("I want to browse") | Concrete listings with historical actuals; scarcity stated as structural fact (one buyer per app) | Countdown/urgency mechanics; fake "sold" badges |

## Trust psychology note

This audience's default prior for "buy an app online, no humans" is **scam until proven otherwise**. Every design choice should be scored against one question: *does this look like something a scammer would never bother to do?* Publishing failure terms, methodology, the full contract, and real Viennese identities are all in that class. Testimonials, urgency, and glossy earnings talk are all in the opposite class — even when technically true, they pattern-match to fraud and are banned anyway.
