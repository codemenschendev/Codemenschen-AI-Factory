# 02 — Brand & Positioning

## Category definition

We are creating a category, not entering one. Working category name:

> **App licensing for backers** — you license an exclusive, validated app; a professional studio builds, promotes, and runs it under an agreed roadmap.

What the category is NOT (say this explicitly, everywhere):
- Not "buy a software business" (that's Acquire.com: whole startups, sophisticated buyers, IP transfer, due diligence). We sell single licensed apps to first-timers.
- Not investing, not a security, not passive income, not equity crowdfunding. [COUNSEL REVIEW: KMG/prospectus boundary — category language must be co-designed with counsel.]
- Not no-code tooling. The buyer never builds anything.

## Positioning statement

> For non-technical people who want hands-on exposure to software and AI but can't code and don't want to hire developers, **Appwerk** is the only place where you can license an exclusive app that real users have already paid for — and have the studio that built it keep developing and promoting it for you. Unlike marketplaces that sell whole startups to experienced operators, every Appwerk app is validated with real paying users, priced between €500 and €5,000, run under a transparent roadmap and retainer, and honest about the fact that it can still fail.

**One-line internal north star:** "License and back your own app — we build, promote, and run it."

## Naming directions

Naming constraint (hard): the name must not promise money, passivity, growth, or returns. It should evoke craft, process, and product.

### Direction A — "Appwerk" (working name)
German "Werk" = work/workshop/factory; reads as craft and machinery.
- **Pros:** Bilingual-friendly (Werk is understood, pronounceable in EN); signals Austrian/German engineering craft — a genuine trust asset; concrete and product-centric, zero financial promise; pairs naturally with sub-brands ("Werkstatt" for the build-in-public log, "Werkbank" for the dashboard).
- **Cons:** "App" prefix is generic and dated in EN markets; possible trademark crowding around App*-names (clear early); slightly workshop-humble — may undersell the curation/exclusivity angle.
- **Verdict:** Strong default. Clears the compliance bar by construction.

### Direction B — Craft/Studio names (e.g. "Werkbank", "Grundwerk", "Appstatt")
Lean fully into the workshop metaphor without the generic "App" prefix.
- **Pros:** More distinctive and trademark-able; ages better than "App*"; the studio framing reinforces "they solved the hard technical part."
- **Cons:** EN discoverability/pronunciation risk; needs more explanatory tagline work; SEO cold start on category terms.

### Direction C — Custody/Backing names (e.g. "Firsthand", "Backstage Apps", "Einhand")
Center the buyer's role: backer of one exclusive thing.
- **Pros:** Communicates one-buyer-per-app exclusivity, the model's most distinctive fact; emotionally closest to "I could license my own app."
- **Cons:** "Backing" edges toward investment vocabulary — every candidate needs a compliance read [COUNSEL REVIEW]; harder to keep bilingual; risk of sounding like crowdfunding (Kickstarter adjacency is a mis-signal: we are not pre-orders).

**Recommendation:** Ship with Appwerk; run trademark + domain clearance; keep Direction B as the rename path if App*-crowding bites. Reject Direction C unless counsel clears the vocabulary.

## Tone of voice

**Principle: the tone IS the compliance strategy.** A calm, precise, engineer-honest voice is what makes zero-traction trust possible.

- **Plain and concrete.** "3 users paid €9 each during a 6-week test" beats any adjective. Numbers are always historical actuals.
- **Symmetrically honest.** Every claim of upside sits next to its risk. "Apps can fail. You can lose what you put in" appears in normal-sized type, in the main flow, not a footnote.
- **Craftsmanlike, not hype.** Vienna workshop, not Silicon Valley launchpad. No exclamation points, no "🚀", no "don't miss out."
- **Respectfully assumptive of intelligence, not expertise.** The reader is smart and financially literate but has never shipped software. Explain mechanics; never talk down; never use dev jargon without a one-line gloss.
- **First person plural, named humans.** "We" = the Codemenschen team in Vienna, with faces and an Impressum.

**Banned vocabulary (all languages):** passive income, own a business, returns, ROI, yield, earn while you sleep, guaranteed, "like stocks/real estate/crypto", limited time, hurry, last chance.
**Approved core phrases:** license and back your own app · validated with real paying users · we build, promote, and run it · one buyer per app · apps can fail — here's exactly what happens if this one does.

**DE tone note:** German copy should be written natively, not translated — Austrian formal-but-warm ("Sie" at launch; revisit "Du" only with evidence). Legal precision in DE is a feature for this audience, not friction.
