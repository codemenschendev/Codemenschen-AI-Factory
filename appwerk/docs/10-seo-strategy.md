# 10 — SEO Strategy (DE + EN)

## Strategic frame

We are creating a category, so almost no one searches for exactly what we sell. SEO therefore has two jobs:
1. **Intercept adjacent-intent searches** (people looking for related things — buying app businesses, micro-SaaS, software exposure without coding) and educate them into our category.
2. **Own the category terms we coin** ("license an app", "App lizenzieren und betreiben lassen") so that when the build-in-public strategy creates demand, we're the definitional result.

**Compliance rule that overrides all keyword opportunity:** if ranking for a keyword requires a page whose promise we can't honestly keep, we drop the keyword or serve it only with educational content that explicitly corrects the searcher's expectation. Rankings are worthless if the landing experience mis-sells — mis-set expectations show up as withdrawals and complaints (01-KPIs).

## Keyword clusters

### Cluster 1 — Category creation (own these; low volume now, definitional later)
- EN: license an app, app license with revenue rights, exclusive app license, back an app, licensed app with managed development
- DE: App lizenzieren, App-Lizenz kaufen, exklusive App-Lizenz, App entwickeln lassen und betreiben lassen
- Pages: homepage, how-it-works, glossary/definition page ("What is an exclusive app license?" / "Was ist eine exklusive App-Lizenz?"). Write the definitional content Wikipedia-grade; we are the reference.

### Cluster 2 — Adjacent purchase intent (intercept + differentiate)
- EN: buy an app business, buy a small SaaS, buy micro-SaaS, websites/apps for sale, Acquire.com alternatives, buy an online business as a beginner
- DE: App kaufen (Business-Intent), Online-Business kaufen, SaaS kaufen, Micro-SaaS kaufen, Webprojekt kaufen
- Content: honest comparison pages — "Buying an app business vs. licensing an app: what's actually different" — that state plainly we do NOT sell businesses or IP, and that Acquire.com-style acquisition may be the better fit for experienced operators. The differentiation IS the compliance: these pages must actively disqualify wrong-fit searchers.

### Cluster 3 — Validated MVP / product validation (build-in-public flywheel)
- EN: validated MVP, MVP validation with paying users, how to validate an app idea, willingness-to-pay testing, pre-revenue vs validated
- DE: MVP validieren, App-Idee validieren, zahlende Nutzer Test, Produktvalidierung
- Pages: the published methodology (07) is the pillar page; Werkstatt log posts (incl. failed validations) are the supporting content. This cluster earns links and expertise signals (E-E-A-T) that lift everything else. Lowest compliance risk, highest authority yield — start here.

### Cluster 4 — "Software exposure without coding" (careful framing)
- EN: get involved in software without coding, alternatives to learning to code, no-code business alternatives, participate in the app economy, own an app without coding ⚠️ (serve this query, but on-page language must be "license", never "own the business")
- DE: eigene App ohne Programmieren ⚠️ (same correction on-page), App besitzen Alternative, Software ohne Programmierkenntnisse
- Content: educational — "You don't need to code (or hire developers) to back an app" mapped to messaging beats 3–4 (03).

### Cluster 5 — HIGH-RISK: educational-content-only, honest framing mandatory
These terms carry income-promise intent. We may address them ONLY with educational articles whose explicit job is to correct the searcher's expectations. Never landing pages, never ads, never meta descriptions implying we fulfill the promise.
- EN: passive income apps ⚠️, app that makes money ⚠️, recurring revenue for individuals ⚠️, buy income-generating website ⚠️, app investing / invest in apps ⚠️ [COUNSEL REVIEW: even educational use of "invest in apps" — KMG sensitivity]
- DE: passives Einkommen App ⚠️, App die Geld verdient ⚠️, in Apps investieren ⚠️ [COUNSEL REVIEW]
- Required article stance (verbatim spirit): "If you're looking for passive income, this article will disappoint you — honestly. Apps require ongoing work (ours, paid via retainer), they can fail, and nothing here is passive or guaranteed. Here's what licensing an app actually involves." If an article can't rank while saying this, we don't want the ranking.

### DROPPED keywords (force misleading claims — do not target in any format)
- "guaranteed income app", "apps that pay you", "best passive income 2026", "make money while you sleep", "turnkey profit app/business", "how much can an app earn per month" (any content answering this becomes a forecast), "app investment returns", DE equivalents ("Geld verdienen im Schlaf", "App Rendite", "sichere Einnahmen App").

## Technical & structural notes

- **DE/EN:** proper hreflang (de-AT/de, en), separate URL paths (/de/, /en/), natively written content per 02 — no machine-translation shortcuts on compliance-sensitive pages.
- **E-E-A-T:** author bylines (real Codemenschen humans), About/Impressum completeness, methodology as citable asset, codemenschen.at cross-domain linking.
- **Listings SEO:** app detail pages indexable with structured data (Product schema; NOT financial-product schema); "Licensed" apps stay indexed as case-study pages (historical record supports Cluster 3).
- **Content ops:** every published page passes the banned-vocabulary check (02) pre-publish; treat it as a lint rule, not a review courtesy.

## Sequencing

1. Launch: Cluster 1 definitional pages + methodology pillar (Cluster 3) + FAQ (long-tail objection queries: "what happens if a licensed app fails" etc.).
2. Months 1–3: Werkstatt log cadence feeding Cluster 3; two Cluster 2 comparison pages.
3. Months 3–6: Cluster 4; first two Cluster 5 educational correctives (post counsel sign-off).
4. Measure: rankings matter less than qualified-visitor rate and downstream comprehension KPIs (01) — traffic that withdraws or complains is negative-value traffic.
