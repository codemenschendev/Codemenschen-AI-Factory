# 01 — Website Strategy

## What the site must do

The website is the entire sales organization. There are no human sales calls, no demos, no follow-up emails from a rep. Every function a salesperson would perform — educating, qualifying, objection-handling, closing, contracting — must be performed by pages, copy, and product UI. This is the single most important design constraint.

**Primary goal:** Convert a non-technical visitor with disposable income into a self-serve buyer of an exclusive app license (€500–5,000 + monthly retainer of 5–10%).

**Secondary goals:**
1. Build durable trust from zero traction via radical process transparency.
2. Educate the market on a category that does not yet exist ("license and back your own app").
3. Capture and nurture visitors not ready to buy (waitlist / new-listing alerts).

## Conversion model

Because the offer is novel, high-consideration, and self-serve, the funnel is **education-first, catalog-second, checkout-last**. Expect multi-session purchase journeys (est. 3–7 visits over 1–4 weeks). Do not optimize for first-visit conversion; optimize for return and depth.

```
Awareness (SEO/content/build-in-public)
  → Homepage (emotional arc → "I want to browse available apps")
    → How it works (mechanics, risk, terms in plain language)
      → App catalog (validation evidence: historical actuals only)
        → App detail page (the real "product page")
          → Checkout (contract summary, [COUNSEL REVIEW] withdrawal right, payment)
            → Buyer dashboard (post-purchase: roadmap, retainer, ad data)
```

**Micro-conversions (the funnel's real currency at launch):**
- Email capture: "notify me when new apps are listed" (one buyer per app makes this natural and honest — never framed as urgency).
- "How it works" completion / scroll depth.
- Catalog browse → app detail view.
- FAQ engagement on the four hard objections (ownership, lock-in, failure, no-humans).

**Nurture:** transactional-style emails only — new listings, build-in-public updates, methodology posts. No drip pressure, no countdowns.

## KPI definitions

Guardrail on KPIs themselves: no KPI may incentivize misleading copy. Conversion-rate optimization is bounded by the compliance guardrails; a variant that wins by implying passive income is a failed variant.

| KPI | Definition | Launch target (directional) |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified visitor | Session with ≥2 pages incl. How-it-works or Catalog | baseline TBD |
| Education completion | ≥75% scroll on How-it-works | ≥30% of qualified visitors |
| Catalog engagement | ≥1 app detail view per session | ≥40% of catalog visitors |
| Waitlist capture | Email signups / qualified visitors | 5–10% |
| Purchase conversion | Purchases / app-detail viewers (multi-session, 30-day attribution) | 0.5–2% at launch; low is expected and fine |
| Withdrawal rate | 14-day withdrawals / purchases | <15%; rising rate = mis-set expectations, audit copy |
| Retainer retention | Buyers current on retainer at month 6 | The real health metric of the whole model |
| Trust proxy | FAQ hard-objection engagement + return-visit rate | monitor, don't target |
| Support-question rate | Pre-purchase questions per 100 detail views | Falling rate = site is doing the sales job |

**Anti-KPIs (never optimize for):** time-pressure conversion, first-visit purchase rate, hiding the retainer or suspension terms to lift conversion.

## Sequencing

1. Launch: Homepage, How-it-works, 3–5 app listings, FAQ, legal, methodology page, waitlist.
2. Month 1–3: build-in-public content engine, SEO foundation (see 10-seo), dashboard v1.
3. Post first resale event: publish the resale case study (actuals only) — the strongest possible trust asset.
