# 09 — Company Presentation ("Who is behind this?")

Purpose: define how the operating company is presented to buyers. For this audience, the company's credibility is the product's collateral — buyers are effectively underwriting Codemenschen's competence and continuity, not just an app.

## The credibility transfer problem

Appwerk (working name) is brand-new with zero traction. Codemenschen.at is not: an operating Viennese web development company with real clients, real projects, and years of history. The messaging job is a clean **credibility transfer**: Appwerk is not a startup with an idea; it is a productization of what Codemenschen already does for clients every day — build, ship, and operate web apps — restructured so individuals can back one.

Core narrative (About page spine):

> "We're Codemenschen, a web development company in Vienna. For years, businesses have paid us to build and run their software. Appwerk is the same craft with a different door: instead of one client commissioning one app, we build apps, prove them with real paying users, and license each one exclusively to a single backer — then keep building and running it, exactly as we do for our agency clients."

This framing does three jobs: explains competence (we do this professionally already), explains motive (objection #6 — our business is building and retainers, not operating a portfolio), and explains the retainer (it's literally our agency service, packaged).

## What to show

- **Named humans.** Founders/leads with real photos, roles, and short bios. The person who writes the build-in-public log signs it.
- **Hard identity facts.** Legal entity, Vienna address, Firmenbuch number, Impressum (ECG-required anyway — display it as a feature, not a footer obligation).
- **Codemenschen track record.** Years operating, selected client work, technologies. Direct link to codemenschen.at both ways (codemenschen.at should link to Appwerk too — one-way linking looks like distancing).
- **The workshop, literally.** Photos of the actual office/team in Vienna. For a DACH audience, "real Betrieb in Wien" outperforms any startup aesthetic.
- **Our economics, plainly.** One honest paragraph: "How we make money: license sales, monthly retainers, and 5% on resales. That's all." Publishing our own revenue model closes the "what's the catch" loop.

## What NOT to say

- No "we're building the future of X" grandiosity — conflicts with the craft tone (02).
- No implication that Codemenschen's client success predicts app success for buyers. Track record proves we can **build and operate**; it must never be framed as evidence that a given app will earn. Keep competence claims and outcome claims strictly separated.
- No team-size inflation, no advisor-logo walls, no "backed by" claims unless literally true and relevant.
- If Codemenschen ever raises capital or the structure changes: disclosed factually in the log, never marketed.

## Handling the asymmetry honestly

Appwerk itself is new, and hiding that would undermine everything else. Approved stance, stated once on the About page:

> "Appwerk is new — you may well be among our first backers. We can't show you a long platform history yet, so we show you everything else instead: our methodology, our contract, our failures, and the company behind it."

This converts our weakest fact into a demonstration of the transparency strategy (see 06, 07).

## Continuity commitment

The "what if you disappear" objection (08, #10) is ultimately about the company. The About page carries the continuity answer once counsel finalizes it [COUNSEL REVIEW: license survival, escrow/successor-operator provisions]. Until finalized, the objection may not be answered with vibes ("we're not going anywhere") — placeholder copy states that a contractual continuity provision is being finalized, dated.

## Voice for company content

Same tone system as 02: plain, craftsmanlike, first-person plural, named humans, DE-native and EN-native (not translated). Company pages are where the Austrian identity is allowed to be most prominent — precision, Handwerk, and legal solidity are the brand's terroir.
